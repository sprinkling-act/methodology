## Analysis method

### v1.6 — August 2026

Structural incompatibilities move to the Full Map, on the same ground as rework time. The element already stated that it is the only finding requiring a view from above; the Baseline, whose single source is the director looking from one position, does not have one, so the list was asking it to contain what it cannot establish. Where the Baseline glimpses an incompatibility it is now recorded as an open question, a status the report already carries. Consequence: the Full Map no longer sells what the Baseline announces delivering. Eight elements still, none removed, this one now carries its tier in its title.

### v1.5 — August 2026

What the Baseline promises is narrowed to what it can establish. It states a position, it does not diagnose: what the director cannot know from where they stand, and what it costs to keep deciding without it. It does not return the teams' uses and does not make visible what the director does not see, it measures the distance separating them from it. The ground is not commercial: Star and Strauss, CSCW 1999, establish that the invisibility of work is an organisational process, so making it visible is an operation nobody can run from inside the organisation.

- **Consequence for the Full Map**: its central deliverable is the gap document, the gap between the measurement taken with the director alone and the one taken with the three or four people who run the work. Without a delivered Baseline there is no term of comparison, so requiring it is a methodological constraint before it is a commercial one.
- **The Baseline report is sealed before the first Full Map interview.** Revised after hearing the teams it would no longer be a comparison but a reconstruction.
- **The gap is attributed line by line, by the date of the element.** Predating the close of the Baseline collection: blind spot, established. Postdating it: drift, a finding in its own right. Carried by a statement alone: unattributed, and marked as such. The close of the collection is dated in the report, distinctly from the readout.
- No validity window is published, and no variation of the deliverable is indexed on a triggering delay, for the same reason no price is.

### v1.4 — August 2026

The unit of analysis changes, and with it the object of this repository. Until now it documented a framework for placing an AI system under the AI Act. The work is now the measurement of the distance between what a director describes of one workflow and what the elements they provide establish. The gate framework becomes the regulatory layer that sits *after* that measurement. The method takes the name **Human in the Map**. The grounds for the change are in [`TRANSITION.md`](TRANSITION.md).

- The single source is stated as a limit rather than hidden as a scope: at the Baseline it is the director, and what escapes them does not become visible through better questioning. That limit now sits in the first principle rather than in a closing section.
- The workflow examined is **the one the director designates**, not one selected by comparing several, which would require the overview the method has just said it does not have.
- Scope fixed at **one named workflow**, bounded in writing before anything starts.
- **Exports, not access.** Nothing installed, no agent on the client side, nothing left behind.
- **Declared first, then checked.** Four evidence statuses on every line, and a gap established only after a second source of a different kind.
- **No score.** The 0-100 band that the earlier scored diagnostic attached to a classification is not carried over.
- Three levels published: Evidence check (free), Baseline (€3,900), Full Map (from €9,900, requires a delivered Baseline). Each level can conclude there is no next step.
- **First-pass acceptance rate and rework minutes moved to the Full Map.** Both rest on a time estimate, and a time estimate has to come from the people who do the work rather than from the person who decides.
- Report structure: still seven parts, but not the same seven. "What the workflow takes" disappears, its substance being the two numbers that just moved, and what remains of it, subscriptions and direct costs that an invoice establishes, joins the findings. "Workflow map" becomes "declared map". "Priorities" becomes "what must be decided", with the owner observed rather than proposed and the deadline drawn from a dated fact of the client's own. A new part records the director's corrections and objections verbatim, including those we did not retain.
- GDPR section: the claim that a survey of AI chains produces the article 30 record is withdrawn. The record is the controller's obligation and its completeness is their liability; a survey documents a chain the record probably ignores and shows what is missing from it. Two legal qualifications move to the conditional, and whether these articles apply to a given use is stated as belonging to the client's counsel.
- Three Baseline outcomes written as conditions observable in advance, rather than as an appreciation formed after reading.
- Index thresholds published before being reached: k = 5, N = 30, N = 100.
- Academic footing stated: Star and Strauss (CSCW 1999), Kahneman et al. (*Science* 2004, Day Reconstruction Method), triangulation, Cheung (Cambridge, May 2026, AI debt), arXiv 2503.18238, Eurostat `isoc_eb_ai`. Each figure carries its reserve at every reuse, not only on first mention.
- Term claimed: **role debt**, not role drift.
- Products removed from this repository because they no longer exist: the Free Diagnostic, the Full Report, and continuous monitoring as previously described.

## AI Act gate framework

### v1.3 — May 2026

Post-Digital Omnibus alignment (provisional trilogue agreement of 7 May 2026, subject to formal adoption by the co-legislators).

- Annex III standalone high-risk binding date updated **2 August 2026 → 2 December 2027** (EP enumeration: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, law enforcement, border management; non-exhaustive).
- Annex I product-embedded safety components binding date updated **2 August 2027 → 2 August 2028**.
- Article 50 transparency unchanged (**2 August 2026 baseline preserved**).
- GenAI watermarking advanced **2 February 2027 → 2 December 2026**.
- Safety-component definition narrowed: AI functions that only assist users or optimise performance no longer automatically face high-risk obligations, if their failure or malfunction does not create health or safety risks.
- Art. 5§1(i) added (provisional, applicable 2 December 2026 pending formal adoption): prohibition of AI systems generating CSAM/NCII content, three-branch architecture (designed-for / placed-without-safeguards / deployer-use).
- Art. 50§1-§4 obligations enriched with explicit application timeline (2 August 2026 new systems · 2 December 2026 transitional for §2 watermarking + §4 deepfake disclosure on systems already on EU market).
- Sleeper segments mapped: PWD × §4 employment platforms, CRA × AI Act IoT-AI.

### v1.2 — May 2026

Art. 5(1) split into absolute (§1(e)/(f)/(g)) and conditional (§1(a)/(b)/(c)/(d)/(h)) prohibitions. Conditional triggers HIGH non-terminal with a legal-verification flag instead of CRITICAL terminal. Art. 5§1(a) wording aligned with the regulation: "causing or likely to cause significant harm". Annex III scoring weighted by domain, structural against operational. GPAI standard = LIMITED 35, GPAI systemic risk = HIGH 80. Scope of the free diagnostic clarified as screening, not legal qualification.

### v1.1 — April 2026

Art. 5§1(d) added, criminal risk profiling. Art. 5§1(h) reference corrected, real-time biometric. Art. 6(1) against 6(2) reference corrected. Art. 50§2 content marking activated. All eight prohibited-practice checks (a-h) covered. High-risk obligations enriched with Art. 9-15. International standards alignment section added (NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001). Method card expanded to 23 pages, OpenTimestamps-anchored: [sprinkling-act/timestamps](https://github.com/sprinkling-act/timestamps).

### v1.0 — March 2026

Framework versioned and published. GPAI systemic-risk gate (Art. 55). Art. 6(3) exemption logic. AI Office guidance on Annex III. Regulatory freeze date: March 2026.

### v0.1 — January 2026

Initial release, aligned with the original AI Act calendar (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Official Journal L of 12 July 2024). Six regulatory gates based on Art. 5, 6, 50, 51 and 53. Article mapping for all Annex III domains.
