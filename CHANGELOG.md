## Analysis method

### v2.0 — August 2026

The method is renamed **Human in the Map** and its object changes. Until now this repository documented a framework for placing an AI system under the AI Act. What Sprinkling Act sells is now the reconstruction of one workflow and the measurement of the distance between what an organisation declares about it and what its own material establishes. The gate framework becomes the regulatory layer that sits *after* that reconstruction.

- Unit of analysis fixed at **one named workflow**, bounded in writing before anything starts.
- **Exports, not access.** Nothing installed, no agent on the client side, nothing left behind.
- **Declared first, then checked.** Four evidence statuses on every line, and a gap established only after a second source of a different kind.
- **No score.** The 0-100 band that the earlier scored diagnostic attached to a classification is not carried over.
- Three levels published: Evidence check (free), Baseline (€3,900), Full Map (from €9,900, requires a delivered Baseline). Each level can conclude there is no next step.
- **First-pass acceptance rate and rework minutes moved to the Full Map.** Both rest on a time estimate, and a time estimate has to come from the people who do the work.
- Three Baseline outcomes written as conditions observable in advance, rather than as an appreciation formed after reading.
- Index thresholds published before being reached: k = 5, N = 30, N = 100.
- Academic footing stated: Star and Strauss (CSCW 1999), Kahneman et al. (*Science* 2004, Day Reconstruction Method), triangulation, Cheung (Cambridge, May 2026, AI debt), arXiv 2503.18238, Eurostat `isoc_eb_ai`. Each figure carries its reserve at every reuse, not only on first mention.
- Term claimed: **role debt**, not role drift.
- Products removed from this repository because they no longer exist: the Free Diagnostic, the Full Report, and continuous monitoring as previously described.

## AI Act gate framework

### v1.3 — May 2026

Post-Digital Omnibus alignment (provisional trilogue agreement of 7 May 2026, subject to formal adoption by the co-legislators).

- Annex III standalone high-risk binding date updated **2 August 2026 → 2 December 2027** (EP enumeration: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, law enforcement, border management; non-exhaustive).
- Annex I product-embedded safety components binding date updated **2 August 2027 → 2 August 2028**.
- Article 50 transparency unchanged (**2 August 2026 baseline preserved**).
- GenAI watermarking advanced **2 February 2027 → 2 December 2026**.
- Safety-component definition narrowed: AI functions that only assist users or optimise performance no longer automatically face high-risk obligations, if their failure or malfunction does not create health or safety risks.
- Art. 5§1(i) added (provisional, applicable 2 December 2026 pending formal adoption): prohibition of AI systems generating CSAM/NCII content, three-branch architecture (designed-for / placed-without-safeguards / deployer-use).
- Art. 50§1-§4 obligations enriched with explicit application timeline (2 August 2026 new systems · 2 December 2026 transitional for §2 watermarking + §4 deepfake disclosure on systems already on EU market).
- Sleeper segments mapped: PWD × §4 employment platforms, CRA × AI Act IoT-AI.

### v1.2 — April 2026

Initial public release aligned with the original AI Act calendar (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Official Journal L of 12 July 2024).
